# Skeptical review: temporal reliability of Laya on CFPB complaints

Evidence cutoff: 2 October 2026. Verdict: **GO, BUT MODIFY**. Personal answer to investing 2–3 months in the exact original design: **NO**.

This is an empirical reliability study with a plausible narrow gap, not a new general calibration problem. A good study needs a harder authentic target, same-initialization controls, independently assessed confidence gates, and a defensible end date. The August test is not viable in the official files inspected.

## What the closest sources establish

**TDM audit.** Its deployment-shift section asks whether confidence remains meaningful under prevalence/workload changes, fallback changes and hosted-model updates. Your frozen-checkpoint, later-data experiment addresses the first part. It does not evaluate changing hosted versions or an end-to-end fallback service. An audit calling a question open is motivation, not proof of publication novelty. [Audit, §7](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32160v1).

**Laya calibration study.** The OOD document track consists of 24 fictional authored documents and 120 claims. It is not naturally later consumer data. It already studies post-hoc calibration and frozen selective escalation, including a failed risk target. The extension is real temporal transfer, not the discovery that a Laya threshold can fail. [Study](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33843v1).

**Jev crash study.** Platt/isotonic mappings are fit on one random narrative half and evaluated on the other, with directions swapped and repeated splits. This is legitimate held-out recalibration within a workload, not calibration and evaluation on identical examples. It does not establish transfer to future calendar periods or changed prevalence, and explicitly treats prevalence change as a limitation. [Methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24052v1).

**OpenJev and follow-ups.** OpenJev's benchmark training and scoring experiments do not duplicate the longitudinal design. However, JEV-as-a-Judge already freezes pilot-derived gates for an extension set and examines calibration transfer. Together they rule out presenting calibration, CE controls or frozen gates themselves as a new TDM evaluation idea. [OpenJev](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.38850v1), [JEV-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26550v1).

**Human Factors Framework.** The main experiment uses 2014–2022 training, 2023H1 validation and 2023H2–2025 testing. An additional quarterly analysis evaluates degradation. Its main outcome is a company-response-based severity proxy, and HFACS reference labels involve LLM distillation. I found no reported probability-calibration transfer, future confidence-gate experiment or executed rolling recalibration study. Direct MDPI access was restricted; the assessment uses indexed primary article text. [Article](https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/17/9/824).

**More serious CFPB overlap.** Remedy-Aware Financial Innovation fits Platt calibration on September–October 2024 and evaluates November–December, including log loss and reliability plots. Its fixed review fractions are not your fixed confidence threshold: top 2% is a capacity policy with a changing numerical cutoff. It is still enough to invalidate “first chronological calibration evaluation on CFPB.” I inspected its methods, not independently reproduced its findings. The September 2026 relief-workload paper further narrows operational novelty, but its inaccessible full methods leave specific transfer details unresolved. [Remedy paper, §§3.1–3.5, 4.5](https://www.americaspg.com/article/4448/pdf/stream), [workload paper abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43069-026-00695-2).

## Answers A–H

**A/B: Novelty and duplication.** The broad question is not novel: temporal probability drift, uncertainty under distribution shift, selective prediction under shift, and online recalibration are established. I did not locate a study combining a frozen Laya checkpoint, authentic chronological complaint cohorts, matched initialization/head controls, and future accepted-error targets with rolling label-budget analysis. That is a qualified search finding, not a proof that no such work exists. Indexing is particularly incomplete for papers days old. The defensible contribution is a controlled deployment-policy audit and evidence about maintenance cost, not “TDM + CFPB” or another demonstration that ECE can increase. [Davis et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29854127/), [Ovadia et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/9547-can-you-trust-your-models-uncertainty-evaluating-predictive-uncertainty-under-dataset-shift.pdf), [Kamath et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.503/), [Gupta and Ramdas](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/gupta23c.html).

**C: CFPB value.** Product classification is crowded; an authentic dated stream remains useful for a different question. Official provenance and administrative labels are strengths. Selection into complaining, consent/publication, templates and policy changes are weaknesses. Results concern the published narrative population, not all financial consumers or verified consumer harm. The CFPB itself cautions that its database is not a statistical sample. [Official database guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/).

**D: Task.** Four broad products may be too easy, but that must be measured rather than asserted. Use TF-IDF logistic regression and keyword-presence strata in a historical-only pilot. If nearly everything already exceeds 90% accuracy, a 90% selective gate has little scientific content. Make the primary task the existing **Issue label within a known product**, initially debt collection, with credit-card issues as replication if historical support permits. Keep product classification as a coarse control. Freeze supported issues and exclusions using training data and the official taxonomy. These remain consumer-selected administrative labels, not an adjudicated semantic truth. Do not substitute generated severity labels. An external replication can use the dated, authentic arXiv or HuffPost classification tasks packaged in Wild-Time. [Official taxonomy](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_consumer_complaint_form_product_issue_options_August_2023_FINAL.pdf), [Wild-Time](https://github.com/huaxiuyao/Wild-Time).

**E: Baseline.** ModernBERT-large is useful but not sufficient to attribute differences to typed decisions. Laya includes additional checkpoint training, an option-conditioned interface and a different head/loss history. Equal downstream examples do not equalize initialization. Compare the Laya typed head against a conventional head initialized from the **same Laya encoder**, under the same CE targets and training budget. Keep ordinary ModernBERT-large as the practical baseline; include TF-IDF. A typed CE-only run is an attribution control, not a novelty claim. Use raw logits; explicitly disable or record shipped option-count temperature transformations. Laya documentation describes a combined loss, so define exactly what “fine-tuned Laya” means. [Laya fine-tuning documentation](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/blob/main/docs/finetune.md), [official notebook](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/tree/main/notebooks), [typed-decision experiments](https://github.com/kotoba-lang/typed-decisions).

**F: Protocol.** Chronology is appropriate, but the original protocol is incomplete. Start September 2023 to avoid straddling the August taxonomy update. Audit exact and near duplicates across time; report operational-stream and unseen-template results separately. Do not rebalance future/calibration class priors for headline reliability. Product-filtering defines a restricted closed-world population, not general complaint routing. Keep model/hyperparameter selection, temperature fitting and gate assessment distinct. A threshold reaching 90% on the same sample that selected it is not a certified 90% guarantee. Use independent assessment and uncertainty intervals, with assumptions about dependence stated. [Database/taxonomy changes](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/), [selective classification risk control](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2017/hash/4a8423d5e91fda00bb7e46540e2b0cf1-Abstract.html).

Also call this **retrospective temporal replay**. A model released in 2026 and evaluated on 2025 documents was not actually deployed in 2024. Unknown pretraining exposure remains a limitation; restricting downstream fine-tuning dates cannot remove it. Record model/tokenizer revisions and investigate disclosed pretraining dates. A hosted Jev run adds server-version uncertainty, so it should be optional and pinned where possible.

**G: Publication policy and original audit.** On 14 August 2026 the CFPB announced the end of discretionary narrative publication. The archive contains previously published narratives; that is not a completeness guarantee for complaints received up to that date. Narrative publication involves consent and processing. June 2026 also brought complaint-process changes. These are possible measurement/process shifts, not automatically genuine changes in language or consumer problems. [August announcement](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-to-cease-discretionary-publication-of-complaint-narratives-and-visualizations/), [archive](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/foia-requests/foia-electronic-reading-room/cfpb-consumer-complaint-database-narratives-archive/), [June announcement](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/the-cfpb-is-correcting-flaws-to-restore-integrity-and-utility-to-the-consumer-complaint-system/).

**H: Five strongest rejection arguments and fixes.**

1. **Old question with a fashionable model.** Fix: estimate future gate failure, differential degradation under controlled initialization, and label cost of maintenance. Add an authentic second task/domain if making broad TDM claims.
2. **Confounded comparison.** Fix: same Laya encoder, conventional versus typed head, CE control, matched truncation and tuning budget; keep ordinary ModernBERT as a separate practical comparator.
3. **Easy task and questionable target.** Fix: existing within-product issue labels, sparse baseline, historical pilot, explicit administrative-label interpretation and error audit.
4. **Apparent drift is publication censoring or template leakage.** Fix: dated/checksummed archive manifest, narrative-availability audit, taxonomy alignment, duplicate/template analysis and defensible end date.
5. **The claimed reliability promise is statistically unsupported.** Fix: independent gate assessment, confidence intervals with dependence accounted for, coverage alongside risk, prespecified tests, and delayed/budgeted recalibration labels.

## Exact revised experiment

**Title:** “Do Frozen Confidence Gates Survive Temporal Shift? A Controlled Audit of Laya on Consumer Complaint Triage.”

**Data/targets:** official CFPB archive; debt-collection Issue prediction as primary, credit-card Issue prediction as replication, four-product prediction as easy-task control. Final classes use historical support and official label definitions. All primary targets are existing administrative labels. For issue tasks, the product is assumed known and supplied consistently.

**Dates:** train September 2023–June 2024; tune July–September 2024; fit temperature in October; select a gate in November; independently assess it in December. Freeze the final design before looking at future performance. If December fails the prespecified criterion, report that failure; do not repeatedly retune against December. Evaluate each quarter in 2025 and 2026Q1; later cohorts follow the availability audit above. Separating months introduces its own short-term drift, which the December assessment usefully tests.

**Models:** (1) Laya typed choice under a documented adaptation recipe, (2) typed CE-only control if (1) uses the combined loss, (3) the same Laya encoder with a conventional CE head, (4) ordinary ModernBERT-large with CE head, (5) TF-IDF logistic regression. Three seeds for core neural comparisons if resources permit. Matched data, token budgets, input evidence and validation effort. The CE-only versus fixed-head pair isolates the interface/head more cleanly than Laya versus a differently pretrained checkpoint.

**Maintenance arms:** raw probabilities; frozen 2024 temperature; rolling preceding-period temperature with frozen weights. Initially use the same historically selected numerical gate for frozen versus rolling temperature so changes measure probability maintenance. Gate reselection, if added, is a separately named fourth arm. Raw/model-specific gates must be selected historically to the same target, not forced to share a numerical cutoff across models. Add a small class-bias/prior-adjustment comparator because a single temperature cannot express arbitrary prior change. Cache logits so calibration experiments do not repeat encoder inference.

**Feedback:** preregister recent-label budgets, for example 100/500/2,000, and a realistic delay. Sampling is from the entire prior stream, not only accepted predictions. Distinguish an internal intake setting, where product/issue labels may already be available, from a public-release setting. Do not use labels before the assumed availability date. Rolling evaluation may consume earlier test periods only according to the previously frozen online protocol; it must not become post-hoc hyperparameter search.

**Primary estimands:** accepted error rate at the frozen gate and coverage, with the excess over a 10% error target; NLL and differential degradation relative to the matched classifier. Report macro-F1, balanced accuracy, Brier, classwise/signed calibration, ECE and reliability diagrams secondarily. Compare models at common coverage as well as their own operational gates. Test-set risk–coverage curves are descriptive; selecting a new cutoff from them is oracle analysis, not deployed performance.

**Uncertainty:** cluster/time-block resampling for paired differences; report seed variability separately. Exact binomial certification assumes independent observations and is inappropriate for unhandled template dependence. Preregister primary endpoints and avoid treating every model × quarter × metric contrast as an independent confirmatory discovery. A small number of quarters supports descriptive drift association, not a causal mechanism claim.

**Mechanism analysis:** class-prior divergence; within-class language/length changes; duplicate/template rates; narrative availability; class-conditional reliability; natural-prior versus standardized-prior sensitivity. Do not interpret one correlation between seven quarterly ECE values and embedding drift as proof of cause.

**Revised RQs:**

1. At a historically assessed error target, how do accepted error and coverage change across subsequent complaint cohorts?
2. Does typed prediction show different temporal degradation from a conventional head under matched initialization, data and adaptation controls?
3. How much prior-period labeled feedback is needed for rolling calibration to maintain the gate, and how sensitive is this to delay?
4. When does scalar temperature adjustment fail relative to a prior-sensitive correction, and is failure associated with class-prior, within-class or publication/template changes?

Two distinctions are essential. Calibration near confidence 0.9 is not the same estimand as accuracy among all predictions above a threshold. Also, positive temperature scaling preserves the predicted class for each example: it can alter confidence and acceptance but cannot repair the model's full-coverage classification errors. In multiclass problems it can change confidence ordering across examples, so do not assume an invariant risk–coverage curve.

## Feasibility, scores and investment decision

The project is feasible with restrained T4 use, cached logits and a small prespecified tuning budget. Do not plan full 421M-parameter training on a 6GB RTX 2060. Official Laya fine-tuning examples target T4-class resources; a two-T4 DDP setup does not combine the cards into one 32GB memory pool. Use the local GPU for smaller inference/experiments and CPU preprocessing. Validate memory on a small batch before committing. Jev API cost/version/access adds scope without being necessary. [Fine-tuning docs](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya/blob/main/docs/finetune.md).

**Final verdict: GO, BUT MODIFY.** The exact original design is too incremental and its final time window is misleading. A controlled, reproducible reliability-maintenance study could support a first empirical paper. A strong general ML conference claim would need more than one application and a new insight or method; an applied venue/workshop is the more plausible initial target.

**Would I invest the next 2–3 months in the exact idea? NO.** I would first run a short historical-data feasibility pilot for the revised issue task and matched control. Proceed only if the gate has a nontrivial risk/coverage tradeoff, sufficiently independent examples, and adequate future-cohort availability. Do not require drift or Laya superiority to appear: a well-powered finding of stability or no difference is valid. The work should be designed to distinguish explanations, not to manufacture a negative result about a new model.
