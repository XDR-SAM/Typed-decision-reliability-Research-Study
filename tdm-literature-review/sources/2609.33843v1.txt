 Laya as a Typed Probabilistic Assessor:
 An Independent Reproduction and a Preregistered Study of
 Calibration and Selective Escalation 
 
 
 Gowthamkumar Nandakishore
 
 September 2026 

 Abstract 
 
 The shipped Laya Typed-Decisions checkpoint, a 421M-parameter ModernBERT-large
assessor that answers typed choice / noul / score questions
over workflow state, is uniformly under -confident. The signed
confidence–accuracy gap is − 0.214 -0.214 , every occupied reliability bin’s accuracy
exceeds its confidence, and that sign uniformity collapses every binned ECE variant
to the same value, 0.214 0.214 . The card frames the risk as over-confidence; the
measured direction is the opposite, and the direction decides which way a
confidence-gated cascade fails. A single disjointly fitted temperature
( T = 0.469 T{=}0.469 , sharpening) removes most of the miscalibration (held-out ECE 0.204 → 0.037 0.204\to 0.037 ) and outperforms the shipped per-option-count table. The frozen
selection rule instead chose isotonic regression, which overfit and failed its
held-out NLL contrast on both tracks, so hypothesis H2 is not supported. Re-running the released
checkpoint on its full official test split reproduces the card’s headline accuracy
( 0.767 0.767 vs. 0.766 0.766 ). The retrospective E1 reproduction preceded the analysis
freeze; E2–E8 were prospectively preregistered, and 20 of 22 executed confirmatory
tests reject under Benjamini–Hochberg FDR at q = 0.05 q{=}0.05 (two descoped). The frozen
gate beats random escalation but misses its 10 % 10\% accepted-set error target on
both tracks, an exploratory out-of-distribution probe finds no zero-shot transfer
(accuracy 0.617 0.617 ), and every score measures agreement with a synthetic teacher
whose self-agreement ceiling ( 0.735 0.735 ) the specialist exceeds. Per-decision
predictions, run manifests, and the frozen preregistration are in the
ancillary files.
The author has no affiliation with the model’s publisher, the dataset’s
publisher, or TypeSafe (§ 13 ). 
 
 
 
 Keywords: typed decisions; language-model evaluation; assessor;
calibration; selective prediction; reproducibility; preregistration 
 
 
 1 Introduction 

 
 Automated assessment increasingly routes real decisions, such as whether an agent
trace needs human review, whether an invoice is paid or held, and whether a security
alert is closed or contained. A typed decision interface serves this setting directly. The model
receives one piece of unstructured state and several typed questions, and returns a
probability distribution over each question’s label space rather than free text. This
differs from reference-based answer grading, where a candidate is compared with a
supplied reference ( Gisserot-Boukhlef et al., 2026 ) , and from general verification, where a solution
or trajectory receives a graded quality score ( Kwok et al., 2026 ) . It is closest in
interface to decision-only judging services ( Li et al., 2026 ) 1 1 
 1 
 
 
 
 Li et al. (2026) 
evaluate the commercial TypeSafe JEV service as a black box; that study is
independent of TypeSafe and of this work. , but the artifact studied
here is small, open-weight, and local. 
 
 
 The gap this paper addresses is the distance between a reported benchmark accuracy and a
deployable confidence signal. The public model card for Laya Typed-Decisions reports
 0.766 0.766 accuracy on its benchmark’s test split ( reported , model
card 2 2 
 2 
 
 
 
 https://huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya-typed-decisions ,
accessed 2026-09-26; revision 1a793eb5 . ). Yet the same
card instructs users to treat the checkpoint’s confidence as uncalibrated, and its
shipped temperature configuration is known to be misconfigured: the temperatures
fitted during training used a slice of the training data ( reported , model
card), and they are overridden at inference by an inherited per-option-count
table whose out-of-range choice:11+ entry is clamped by laya 
0.3.20 ( measured ). Whether such an artifact can act as a trustworthy
first-stage assessor (accepting decisions when confident and escalating when unsure,
as Li et al. (2026) frame it for a proprietary hosted judge) is an empirical question
that its card does not answer. 
 
 
 This is an independent study. The author has no affiliation with Convai
Innovations, which publishes the model, its base model, and the laya 
package; with the LocalLLaMA organization, which publishes the benchmark; or
with TypeSafe (§ 13 ). What the study supplies is a frozen protocol,
frozen thresholds, and per-decision artifacts that let anyone check or extend
the numbers. A previously
circulated draft of this work was a protocol-only manuscript that proposed the
evaluation and reported no measurements. This version executes that protocol in two
registers. The reproduction (E1) ran first and is retrospective, predating the
preregistration under the plan’s fit-nothing exemption. The plan was then
frozen, and every prospectively frozen analysis (E2–E8) executed against the
frozen thresholds; only those analyses are claimed as preregistered.
§ 3.7 states the timing, including the acceptance-gate item it fails,
and Appendix  D gives the full execution timeline. The one descoped
component is the external-validity set (E9; § 9 ). 
 
 
 Contributions. 

 
 
 1. 
 
 A decomposition of the card’s miscalibration (E4). The shipped
checkpoint is uniformly under-confident (signed gap − 0.214 -0.214 ), not over-confident as
the card’s guidance implies; one disjointly fitted temperature removes almost all of
it (held-out ECE 0.204 → 0.037 0.204\to 0.037 ), and the fitted temperature sharpens 
( T = 0.47 T{=}0.47 ) where the shipped override softens ( T = 1.25 T{=}1.25 – 1.98 1.98 ). The
inherited configuration is directionally backwards on this benchmark. 
 
 2. 
 
 An escalation analysis with explicit scope limits (E5–E6). No
runnable strong judge exists in this study’s environment (an author-directed
constraint in the frozen plan), so cascade end-to-end accuracy is reported only as an
 oracle-escalation upper bound with the escalation target’s accuracy and cost
as explicit symbolic parameters, alongside a random-escalation control at matched
rate. The confidence signal does real work (E1t/E2t, + 6.5 +6.5 / + 7.6 +7.6 accepted-set
accuracy points over random escalation, both FDR-significant), yet the frozen gate
misses its 10 % 10\% error target out-of-sample and passes confidently wrong security
decisions (§ 7 ). The proprietary JEV service is never run (no API access
exists) and appears only as third-party context with its source’s own disclaimer. 
 
 3. 
 
 A preregistered analysis protocol, frozen after the reproduction and
before all fitting. 
All thresholds, temperature-fitting procedures, split assignments, confidence
statistics, hypothesis operationalizations, and a 24-test false-discovery-rate
family were frozen (2026-09-26, 15:50 UTC) before
any fitting touched the test split, and the frozen choices were then followed even
where they hurt; the frozen method-selection rule picked an overfit calibrator on
both tracks (§ 6 ). The design
separates a split-half protocol on the released checkpoint (Track A) from a clean
re-training replication (Track B), because the released checkpoint’s calibration
data is contaminated by construction (§ 3.3 ). 
 
 4. 
 
 An independent reproduction of the released checkpoint (E1). We
re-run it on all 400 test cases and report every headline metric against
the card’s reported values with 95% BCa confidence intervals from a case-level 
cluster bootstrap (the 2,000 decisions are five-per-case and correlated;
decision-level intervals would be too narrow, Appendix  E ). Soft accuracy is the one headline
metric that fails to reproduce ( 0.509 0.509 vs. 0.471 0.471 ; CI excludes the reported
value) and is recorded as an open reproducibility finding (§ 4 ). We
release the per-decision prediction artifact (2,000 rows); the model card
publishes no per-decision record behind its headline numbers. Per-decision
artifacts for every headline run are released as well. 
 
 5. 
 
 A clean replication that prices contamination (Track B). Re-training

 2 Related work 

 
 Reference-based encoder assessment. 

 
 Gisserot-Boukhlef et al. (2026) show across 36 models and 15 tasks that lexical answer matching
correlates poorly with human judgment, and train an encoder (EuroBERT-210M;
 Boizard et al., 2025 ) on question–candidate–reference triplets to judge correctness
in reference-based settings. Two of their findings frame our study. Their
encoder’s soft probabilities are usably calibrated after a single temperature
( τ = 1.75 \tau{=}1.75 ). And their cost-effective fallback cascade , regex
grading first with the encoder only where regex fails, cuts total compute roughly
 5 × 5\times when regex fails on 20% of items ( third-party ; their benchmarks). Laya
Typed-Decisions descends from the same encoder lineage (it is a ModernBERT-large
checkpoint; Warner et al., 2024 ), but its task is not reference-based; the benchmark
supplies no reference answers, so BERT-as-a-Judge is not runnable here and none of its
numbers can be compared to ours (preregistration §0.2). 
 
 
 
 Confidence-gated cascades. 

 
 Selective prediction descends from the reject option ( Chow, 1970 ) through
selective deep networks ( Geifman and El-Yaniv, 2017 ) . For language models, FrugalGPT sequences
cheaper models before expensive ones ( Chen et al., 2024 ) , and
 Jitkrittum et al. (2023) characterize when confidence-based deferral is sufficient in
principle. Closest to us, JEV-as-a-Judge ( Li et al., 2026 ) studies a
decision-only judge whose maximum label probability gates escalation to a stronger
generative judge. Their frozen cascade at threshold 0.9 0.9 accepts 53.7 % 53.7\% of items and
retains ∼ 99 % {\sim}99\% of the strong judge’s accuracy at ∼ 57 {\sim}57 – 62 % 62\% of its fee
( third-party ; their benchmarks). Three facts matter for reading this paper against
theirs. JEV is a proprietary hosted service evaluated as a black box; its
benchmarks are five public judge suites, none of them the four workflow domains
studied here; and its authors have never evaluated it on those domains .
We run nothing against JEV (no API access exists; the frozen plan forbids any JEV
run or estimate) and make no head-to-head claim. 
 
 
 
 Fine-grained verification. 

 
 LLM-as-a-Verifier ( Kwok et al., 2026 ) computes continuous quality scores as expectations
over scoring-token logits and scales verification along granularity, repetition, and
criteria decomposition, reporting state-of-the-art results on agentic benchmarks
(Terminal-Bench V2 86.5 % 86.5\% , SWE-Bench Verified 78.2 % 78.2\% , RoboRewardBench 87.4 % 87.4\% ,
MedAgentBench 73.3 % 73.3\% ; third-party ). Laya’s interface is materially different (a trained
encoder emitting distributions over typed label spaces rather than
scoring-token readout from a generative model), but the shared lesson is that
probability shape beyond the argmax carries the operational signal. 
 
 
 
 Calibration and selective prediction measurement. 

 
 Modern networks are often miscalibrated and temperature scaling is the standard
post-hoc remedy ( Guo et al., 2017 ) . Binned ECE is a biased estimator; bin placement
changes the number ( Nixon et al., 2019 ; Roelofs et al., 2022 ) , and recalibrated models can be less
calibrated than they report ( Kumar et al., 2019 ) . We therefore report an ECE family (equal-width at several
bin counts, equal-mass, classwise, and a signed confidence–accuracy gap) with proper
scoring rules ( Gneiting and Raftery, 2007 ) rather than a single ECE. Judge-style evaluators
also carry presentation biases, in particular position and order sensitivity
 ( Zheng et al., 2023 ; Zheng et al., 2024 ; Pezeshkpour and Hruschka, 2024 ) , which motivates the
frozen robustness suite (E7). On
statistics, we follow bootstrap practice for clustered data
 ( Efron and Tibshirani, 1993 ; Davison and Hinkley, 1997 ) , Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control ( Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995 ) ,
and preregistration norms ( Nosek et al., 2018 ) . 
 
 
 
 
 3 Setup 

 
 3.1 Model 

 
 Laya Typed-Decisions is a 421M-parameter typed-decision checkpoint built on
ModernBERT-large ( Warner et al., 2024 ) , released by Convai Innovations
(footnote  2 ). It answers three primitives: choice 
(distribution over N N labels), noul (probability a statement is true), and
 score (distribution over ordered rubric levels plus an expected score). Per
its card ( reported ), it was fine-tuned from the convaiinnovations/laya base with
an RLCD-style recipe: the policy emits a distribution over typed options, and
exploration adds zero-mean Gaussian noise to logits ( σ \sigma annealed
 → 0.1 0.4\!\to\!0.1 ). The reward
is a strictly proper scoring rule (log plus 0.75 ⋅ 0.75\cdot spherical, plus a ranked
probability score term for ordinal score questions); updates are REINFORCE
with a group-mean baseline plus soft cross-entropy against teacher distributions. At
inference the shipped configuration applies a per-option-count temperature table
( choice:3-5 = 1.760 =1.760 , noul:2 = 1.983 =1.983 , score:3-5 = 1.251 =1.251 ,
…; measured , read from the pinned checkpoint) which overrides per-type
temperatures fitted during training, temperatures that were themselves fitted on a
slice of the training data ( reported , model card). The card’s own
instruction is to treat shipped confidence as uncalibrated. We evaluate the checkpoint
exactly as shipped, bug and all, through laya 0.3.20 predict_batch . 
 
 
 
 3.2 Benchmark and the teacher-label caveat 

 
 The Typed Decisions benchmark 3 3 
 3 
 
 
 
 https://huggingface.co/datasets/LocalLLaMA/typed-decisions ,
accessed 2026-09-26; revision f7a2487e . The dataset is independent of, and
unaffiliated with, TypeSafe. The LocalLLaMA organization on Hugging Face that
publishes it is distinct from the Reddit community of the same
name. has four workflow subsets
( agent_trace_observability , customer_service ,
 invoice_processing , security_incidents ), each with 300 train and
100 test cases; every case asks five typed questions over one shared state, giving
6,000 train and 2,000 test decisions. Option criteria descriptions are
part of the input. Gold labels are the mean of three samples from a teacher
endpoint (roughly 4B-class capability, temperature 0.7; reported , dataset card); a
 label_agreement field records how much the samples disagreed. 
 
 
 Three dataset-card reference points ( reported ) anchor interpretation: a majority
baseline scores 0.520 0.520 (on the combined 1,600 cases), a model with perfect knowledge
of the latent generating factors scores 0.704 0.704 , and teacher self-agreement ,
a fresh teacher sample scored against gold built from the others, scores 0.735 0.735 .
The card’s own reading is that “a score much above 0.75 means a model has learned the
teacher’s quirks rather than the task.” Agreement with a teacher is not correctness
 ( Gudibande et al., 2023 ) ; the dataset card gives a concrete example where the teacher
missed a duplicate invoice. Every number in this paper measures teacher agreement on
synthetic cases, and § 10 returns to what that does and does not
support. The card also separates specialist models (fitted on these four
workflows) from generalist ones (zero-shot on unseen schemas) and warns the two
are not comparable rows; we preserve that grouping everywhere. 
 
 
 
 3.3 Split design (Track A and Track B) 

 
 The released checkpoint was fine-tuned on all 1,200 training cases, so any
temperature or threshold fitted on training data is contaminated, the
defect its card admits. The frozen design therefore runs two
tracks and reports both. 
 
 
 Track A (split-half test protocol; released checkpoint). The 400 official
test cases are partitioned at case level, stratified by workflow (50/50 per workflow),
into calib (200 cases, 1,000 decisions) and eval (200 cases,
1,000 decisions), with generator and seed fixed in the preregistration (NumPy PCG64,
seed 20260926; ID lists in track_a_split.json , SHA-256
 7719b147… ). Every temperature and threshold is fitted on calib 
only; all Track A headline results are reported on eval only. eval 
results are not directly comparable to the card’s 2,000-decision figures (half the
sample); the full-400 reproduction (E1) is reported separately and labeled as such,
not as a calibrated result. 
 
 
 Track B (clean replication). We fine-tune our own specialist from the pinned
 laya base model with the card’s RLCD recipe (§ 3.1 ),
splitting the 1,200 training cases at case level into 960 fine-tune / 240 validation
(stratified, seed 20260926). Calibration and cascade thresholds are fitted only on the
240 validation cases; the 400-case test split is evaluated exactly once. Track A
answers whether the released artifact can be used responsibly today; Track B answers
whether the method works when the protocol is clean. Hypotheses H2/H3 are primarily
tested on Track B.

 3.6 Uncertainty quantification and multiplicity 

 
 The 2,000 test decisions sit in 400 case clusters (five decisions per case, same
document and context), so decisions are not independent. Every confidence interval in
this paper is a case-level cluster bootstrap : resample cases with
replacement, keep each case’s five decisions together, n boot = 10,000 n_{\mathrm{boot}}=10{,}000 ,
seed 0; BCa intervals primary for single-quantity metrics, percentile as robustness
check ( Efron and Tibshirani, 1993 ; Davison and Hinkley, 1997 ) . Model comparisons use paired differences on
identical resampled case sets; paired-difference intervals are percentile (BCa on a
difference needs a combined jackknife and was not preregistered for the paired case).
Bootstrap p-values use the finite-sample add-one convention ( b + 1 ) / ( B + 1 ) (b{+}1)/(B{+}1) , doubled
for two-sided tests, so zero exceedances in B = 10,000 B{=}10{,}000 resamples is reported as
 p ≤ 10 − 4 p\leq 10^{-4} (one-sided) or p ≤ 2 × 10 − 4 p\leq 2{\times}10^{-4} (two-sided), the resolution
limit instead of zero. Naive i.i.d. decision-level intervals appear only in a
frozen appendix contrast (Figure  8 ). Confirmatory inference is
limited to a frozen family of 24 tests with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control at
 q = 0.05 q=0.05 ( Benjamini and Hochberg, 1995 ) ; everything else is exploratory and labeled as such.
Under the preregistration’s §0.a scope amendment, the two external-validity family
members (F1t/F2t) could not execute in this version and are reported as not executed;
the BH denominator conservatively remains the full family size of 24 (equivalent to
assigning them p = 1 p{=}1 ). Twenty of the 22 executed tests reject
(Appendix  E ).

under-confidence. Whether the raw pre-temperature logits are over-confident,
i.e. how much of it is configuration artifact versus model, is
the frozen E4 question (§ 6 ).
For cascade deployment the direction
matters. Uniform under-confidence inflates escalation rates (costly) rather than
concealing errors (unsafe); the measured operating consequences appear in § 7 .

 10 Discussion 

 
 Benchmark circularity, and what 0.767 0.767 can and cannot mean. 

 
 The measured accuracy ( 0.767 0.767 ; measured ) sits above the teacher’s own
self-agreement ceiling ( 0.735 0.735 ; reported ) and well above the perfect-factor-knowledge
reference ( 0.704 0.704 ; reported ). The dataset card’s own interpretation rule says scores
much above ∼ 0.75 {\sim}0.75 indicate a model has learned the teacher’s idiosyncrasies
rather than the task. The correct reading of E1 is therefore that a
421M specialist fine-tuned on these four workflows saturates agreement with a noisy
synthetic teacher on held-out cases from the same generator; no “beats the
teacher” reading survives that ceiling. Teacher agreement is not
correctness ( Gudibande et al., 2023 ) ; a model can be penalized for being right where
the teacher is wrong.
This is also why no superiority claim over JEV is available from any table here. The
third-party JEV row ( 0.727 0.727 ; third-party ) was produced by a different party under different
prompts and pipeline on a benchmark whose ceiling is 0.735 0.735 . The specialist/generalist
grouping makes the rows non-comparable by the dataset card’s own discipline.
A zero-shot hosted model on the same card also clears the ceiling ( 0.768 0.768 ;
 third-party ), so the ceiling is a reading rule for a specialist fitted to this teacher
rather than a hard cap on any model; the claim here concerns only what the
specialist’s score can mean. 
 
 
 
 Direction of miscalibration is an operational quantity. 

 
 Two checkpoints with ECE 0.21 0.21 can fail in
opposite directions. An over-confident assessor silently passes errors through a
cascade gate, while an under-confident one escalates too much and erodes the cost
case. The shipped Laya artifact is the second kind (§ 4.2 ), with the
mechanism measured in § 6 . After proper calibration the signed gap flips
to slightly positive ( + 0.028 +0.028 for the single temperature and + 0.033 +0.033 for
the selected isotonic map on eval ), mild over-confidence, which
is the regime in which the E5 gate under-delivers. Direction of
miscalibration, not its magnitude, determines which cascade failure mode a deployment
gets, and the threshold was chosen on the calibrator’s own fitting data, so its
in-sample accepted-set error understated the held-out error (the § 7 
decomposition). 
 
 
 
 Procedure-level failures the preregistration recorded. 

 
 One frozen design choice produced both recorded failures. The calibrator and the
threshold were fitted on the same split, under an NLL-based selection rule that
favors expressive calibrators, so the selected isotonic map overfit (A6/C6). The
same in-sample optimism let τ ∗ \tau^{*} clear a target it could not hold out of
sample (H3a; decomposition in § 7 ). Without preregistration both
would have been silently “fixed” (swap in the vector method, nudge τ \tau to
 0.75 0.75 ) and the paper would have reported only successes. The recorded failures
measure the generalization gap of the procedure , which is what a
practitioner reusing this recipe on their own
200-case calibration set needs to know ( Nosek et al., 2018 ) . The reusable lesson is to
cross-fit (fit the calibrator on one half of the fitting split, choose τ \tau on
the other) or to select τ \tau by an upper confidence bound on the fitting-split
error. 
 
 
 
 Where a compact typed assessor is the right tool. 

 
 The measured operating point, ∼ 19 {\sim}19  ms per decision at batch 1 on a
laptop-class accelerator with full distribution outputs ( measured ; § 4 ),
is the argument for the interface. It is local, auditable, and cheap enough to run on
every decision, with typed label spaces fixed by the workflow. Published cascades in adjacent settings
 ( Li et al., 2026 ; Gisserot-Boukhlef et al., 2026 ; Chen et al., 2024 ; Jitkrittum et al., 2023 ) suggest such a first
stage can carry most of a workload when its confidence orders its errors , and
that thresholds must be validated locally. This study now has the measured version of that advice. The
confidence signal beat random escalation at matched rate on both tracks, and
calibration is what made the gate’s coverage usable at all (E8).
In-distribution, the measured
posture is that a compact typed assessor plus one fitted temperature is a useful
triage stage at roughly two-thirds coverage. That holds provided the error target is
treated as an estimate to monitor, not a guarantee, and the accepted set is audited
(§ 7 ). Out of distribution, the exploratory probe measured no transfer
(§ 9.1 ); nothing in this paper counters that. 
 
 
 
 
 11 Limitations 

 
 
 1. 
 
 Single checkpoint, single configuration. One released artifact at one
pinned revision, as shipped; no model-family sweep, no seed variation. 
 
 2. 
 
 Synthetic, teacher-labeled benchmark. Gold is the mean of three
samples from one ∼ {\sim} 4B-class teacher; every metric is agreement with that
teacher rather than correctness ( Gudibande et al., 2023 ) ; cases are template-generated,
with no fielded deployment behind them. 
 
 3. 
 
 No compute-matched architectural comparison. Comparators differ in
size, training exposure, and interface; grouping by task access mitigates but does
not remove this. 
 
 4. 
 
 No TypeSafe JEV access. All JEV figures are third-party on different
pipelines and samples; nothing here supports a head-to-head ranking in either
direction. 
 
 5. 
 
 No preregistered external validity set in this version (descoped;
§ 9 ); every confirmatory conclusion is restricted to the synthetic
benchmark, and H4 goes untested. The E10 probe (§ 9.1 ) is exploratory,
post-registration, self-authored (120 claims, 24 documents), and its claims are
correspondingly weak, a transfer-failure signal rather than a measurement of
external validity. 
 
 6. 
 
 No independent annotator pool. The external-set annotation protocol
(two blinded annotators, adjudication, agreement statistics) exists only in the
unreleased pre-freeze plan; no human adjudication of any model output occurred
in this version. 
 
 7.
